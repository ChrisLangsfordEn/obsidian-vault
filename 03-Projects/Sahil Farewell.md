1. The Opening (Hook & Recognition)

- **Acknowledge the Occasion:** Start by stating the purpose of the gathering—bidding farewell to a valued team member.
- **Set the Tone:** Keep it warm, appreciative, and positive.
- **Example:** _"Hello everyone. As most of you know, we are gathered here to say goodbye to [Name], who is moving on to an exciting new chapter."_ 

2. The Body (Impact & Accomplishments)

- **Highlight Key Contributions:** Mention specific projects, successes, or milestones they achieved during their time in your team.
- **Focus on Their Growth:** Note how they developed as a professional and the value they added.
- **Share Anecdotes:** Briefly recount a memorable, positive, or lighthearted moment that illustrates their personality or work ethic.
- **Focus on the Human Element:** Speak to their character—reliability, creativity, or how they helped improve team morale.
- **Example:** _"I still remember when [Name] took the lead on the [Project Name] under a tight deadline. Their ability to stay calm and organized was invaluable to us, and it truly set a new standard for our team."_ 

3. Personal Appreciation (The "Thank You")

- **Express Gratitude:** Thank them specifically for their hard work and commitment.
- **Acknowledge Their Legacy:** Mention what they are leaving behind (e.g., mentorship, a new process, or a positive, friendly atmosphere).
- **Example:** _"[Name], I want to personally thank you for your dedication. It has been a true privilege to lead you and to watch you grow."_ 

4. The Future & Closing (Well Wishes)

- **Offer Encouragement:** Express confidence in their future success.
- **Final Well Wishes:** Wish them luck in their next adventure.
- **Close with Positivity:** A brief, uplifting statement to leave a lasting positive impression.
- **Example:** _"While we will miss you dearly, I am excited to see the amazing things you will do next. We wish you nothing but the best in your new role!"_ 

Tips for Delivery

- **Keep it Short:** Aim for 2 to 3 minutes; generally, 4–8 minutes is considered the maximum for a formal speech.
- **Be Sincere:** Avoid generic, cookie-cutter praise. Specific stories make the speech more memorable.
- **Maintain Composure:** It is fine to be emotional, but use notes to help you stay on track.
- **Focus on Them:** Ensure the speech focuses on their achievements and future, rather than just your feelings or company politics.
