This is an entry-level role and may be capable developers but in general, lack the experience required to make informed decisions. Time is important here; with time a Software Engineer will learn: 

- Good coding principles
    
- How to deal with and treat customer
    
- How to work with other developers and software professionals
    

Because the Software Engineer has not yet built up enough depth of experience - we know they are still learning, and thus there are risks associated with his / her deliverables which must be managed. The Software Engineer: 

- must prove that he / she is able to complete micro-tasks in the given amount of time at an acceptable level of quality.
    
- must display a positive attitude towards:
    
    - Actively learning and maximising the opportunities given to them to learn as much as possible
        
    - Work ethic and going the extra mile when necessary to pull themselves up to the level of his / her more senior peers
        
    - Personal growth across different areas of projects (coding, but also willingness to understand specifications, showing the ability to take bigger tasks and break them down themselves, demonstrating the correct level of doing things themselves and asking questions, showing signs of being self-managing)
        
    - Demonstrating a willingness to be involved in various initiatives that take him / her outside his / her comfort zone (training presentations, asking for extra work, seeing where else he / she can be useful).

## So what should a Software Engineer focus on?



When it comes to growth and assessing performance for Software Engineers at this level, we focus on three core categories which you may see are similar to our three core values. These categories reflect how an individual grows in their craft, how they show up for others, and the impact their work has beyond themselves.

Together, they provide a holistic view of performance. Strong performance does not mean perfection in all areas, but it does require consistent evidence that the individual is developing in line with the expectations of an entry-level Software Engineer.

These categories are intentionally broad. They are designed to guide reflection and discussion during reviews, rather than to act as a checklist.

#### Competence in Craft

This individual is focused on learning and gaining competence in their given craft.

At a task level, this individual is not expected to be self-managed or make informed decisions. They are learning on the job and will have their deliverables managed by their more senior peers. This individual can complete micro tasks at an acceptable level of quality, in a pre-defined amount of time. They are expected to do an acceptable job of troubleshooting and debugging their own work.

This individual will know when they have reached the limits of their craft ability and will reach out for help when stuck. They will always attempt to solve their tasks on their own before requesting help and be able to clearly explain what they are struggling with. This individual has knowledge of best practices at the micro level. This individual can apply past learnings to new problems and avoid repeating similar mistakes.

This individual can learn and pickup new frameworks, methodologies, or tools relevant to their craft with the assistance of others. Most of their learnings occur on the job. Other Seniors or Leads will direct and coach this individual in finding project level opportunities to step out of their comfort zone and learn a new aspect of their craft, both technically and through soft skills. 

#### Responsibility to Others

This individual’s responsibility to others is to be an active team member. They are expected to manage their team members' expectations by giving regular detailed status updates (verbally or written) to their more senior peers. During meetings, they will contribute when they have something valuable to say. This individual maintains a calm demeanour and does not let personal circumstance impact their decision-making ability and delivery. This individual does not pigeon-hole their value contribution based on personal preferences.

This individual will make a concerted effort to avoid misunderstandings through their communication. They do not avoid communicating issues to their respective Leads when things have gone wrong. They will show a sense of urgency to all team communications and will respect other people’s points of view.

This individual is known to have a positive outlook and do not perpetuate negative sentiment in the team. They willingly contribute to their team (regardless of responsibility) during a time of crisis or when they can add value. This individual contributes to the overall team identity and culture. 

#### Impact on Customer Success

This individual derives value from their work being used in production and benefitting the customer. They understand that their job is about their craft contribution. They are aware of the how their deliverables serve the customer's needs. When there is a lack of clarity in tasks, this individual does not blame others but pursues all collaborative avenues to find it.

This individual makes a customer feel comfortable because their intent is to achieve the same goals. Wherever they can add value to a customer, they look to get involved.

## How to use these categories in reviews


When responding to the review questions under each category, the focus should be on **observable behaviours, deliverables, and growth over time**, not intent alone.

“Meeting expectations” means the individual is **reliably demonstrating the behaviours and outcomes appropriate for this level**, with support and guidance from more senior peers. “Not meeting expectations” indicates clear gaps that **persist despite feedback, support, or time in role**. “Over and above expectations” is when an individual is demonstrating the behaviours and outcomes appropriate for this level **without much support, and taking on additional tasks / behaviours** once their required tasks are addressed. 

The sections that follow provide more detailed expectations and examples. These are included to help conceptualise what each category looks like in practice and to create a shared understanding of what “meeting expectations” means for this role.

## What “Meeting Expectations” looks like in practice

The detailed expectations below expand on the three review categories by describing how they typically show up day-to-day through technical delivery, communication, reliability, professionalism, learning, and ownership.

They are not intended to introduce new criteria, but rather to:

- Clarify what good performance looks like at this level
    
- Make it easier to answer the longer review questions with concrete examples
    
- Provide a consistent reference point for both self-reviews and team reviews
    

Reviewers and Software Engineers should use these expectations as supporting context when assessing performance within each of the three main categories.

#### Technical Ability and Craft

A Software Engineer at this level is expected to deliver assigned micro-tasks to an acceptable technical and quality standard within the agreed timeframes. Code should follow agreed coding standards, make correct use of source control, and demonstrate an understanding of basic software engineering principles. Deliverables should be reasonably well tested before being released.

They are expected to troubleshoot and debug their own work to a reasonable degree, apply previous learnings to new problems, and avoid repeating the same mistakes. They should understand when they are stuck and follow the agreed escalation approach (for example, attempting a solution first and then asking for help within a reasonable time window, such as the 45-minute rule).

A Software Engineer is not expected to make high-level design decisions but should show growing awareness of best practices and why they exist. With guidance, they can learn and apply new tools, frameworks, or methodologies relevant to their role.

- **Not meeting expectations looks like:** poor or inconsistent use of source control, repeated ignorance of basic best practices, releasing poorly tested code, an inability to troubleshoot issues expected at this level, or remaining stuck without seeking help through the appropriate channels.
    

#### Attitude, Ownership, and Work Ethic



This role requires a constructive and professional attitude. A Software Engineer is expected to take accountability for their work, do what is asked of them reliably, and show willingness to put in extra effort when genuinely required (for example, during critical deadlines or deployments).

They should show initiative at an appropriate level: looking for small ways to help when capacity allows, volunteering for additional work when teams are under pressure, and being open to opportunities that stretch them slightly beyond their comfort zone. They are expected to contribute positively to team morale and culture.

- **Not meeting expectations looks like:** doing the bare minimum only, avoiding accountability for issues within their control, being unreliable when support is needed, or consistently displaying or spreading negative sentiment within the team.
    

#### Deadlines and Delivery



Meeting commitments is critical. A Software Engineer is expected to complete tasks and deliverables as agreed with their Team Lead or Customer, within defined timelines. Where risks arise, they should communicate early, manage expectations, and participate in negotiating realistic extensions if necessary.

They should build a reputation for being dependable and for “getting things done,” whether this relates to sprint tasks, project work, or timely responses to bug fixes.

- **Not meeting expectations looks like:** repeatedly missing agreed deadlines (more than once without valid extenuating circumstances) or failing to communicate delivery risks early.
    

#### Responsibility to Others and Communication



This individual’s responsibility to others is to be an active and reliable team member. They are expected to keep their Team Lead and relevant team members up to date on progress, risks, and blockers through clear, timely communication (written or verbal). They should contribute meaningfully in meetings when they have something of value to add.

They are expected to communicate professionally, show urgency where appropriate, avoid misunderstandings, and escalate issues when things go wrong rather than avoiding them. Respect for different viewpoints and a calm demeanour are important, even under pressure.

- **Not meeting expectations looks like:** only communicating when prompted, slow or absent responses to messages, poor communication leading to repeated misunderstandings, or avoiding communication when issues arise.
    

#### Customer Focus and Professionalism


A Software Engineer should understand that their work ultimately serves the customer. They are expected to show interest in the customer's domain, behave professionally in all customer interactions (communication, punctuality, etiquette, and attention to detail), and seek clarity rather than assigning blame when requirements are unclear.

They should look for appropriate opportunities to add value to the customer and align their intent with the customer’s goals.

- **Not meeting expectations looks like:** being unreachable when needed, demonstrating poor professionalism, or receiving repeated negative feedback from customers.
    

#### Learning, Training, and Growth



Growth is expected over each review period. This includes making use of available learning channels such as on-the-job coaching, online resources, certifications, internal training, or technical forums. With guidance, Software Engineers may contribute to learning initiatives such as internal presentations, Yoda articles, or tech accelerator projects.

They are expected to show concrete progress in areas highlighted in previous performance discussions.

- **Not meeting expectations looks like:** little to no evidence of skill development, avoiding learning opportunities, or failing to address previously identified development areas.
    

#### Timekeeping and Administration


Professional administration is part of the role. This includes accurate and on-time timesheet capturing, attending scheduled meetings, and maintaining reliable working hours. Software Engineers should manage their time responsibly and support the team when required.

- **Not meeting expectations looks like:** frequent missed timesheet capturing deadlines, unclear or questionable time entries, repeated late or missed meetings without notice, or a reputation for clock-watching.
