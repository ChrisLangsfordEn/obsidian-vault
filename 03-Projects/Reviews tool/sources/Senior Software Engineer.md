​​​Senior Software Engineers are experts in their chosen technology stacks, expected to mentor other senior professionals and are amazing troubleshooters. They have run into most problems before and have a very good idea of where to look first when something goes wrong.

They are experienced in Software Engineering and have experienced different situations (good and bad, technical and non-technical) and know exactly why some situations and technical decisions are to be avoided, they know at least one and often several languages at a detailed level and have the ability to learn and pick up new frameworks and technologies as required on their own accord with minimal assistance from others.

At this stage, Seniors who are working towards leadership should be able to run a small project / sub-project (> 4-month project) with minimal assistance, to an acceptable level of quality and within the timeframes that he / she is given

The Senior Software Engineer should be solution-focused I.e. he / she must show an active interest and gain an understanding of the business domain, business objectives and the goals of the project in the context of the customer's business.

## So what should a Senior Software Engineer focus on?

[](https://entelectza.sharepoint.com/sites/entelectintranet/SitePages/SeniorSoftwareEngineer.aspx#so-what-should-a-senior-software-engineer-focus-on "Permalink for So what should a Senior Software Engineer focus on?")

When it comes to growth and assessing performance for Senior Software Engineers at this level, we focus on three core categories which you may see are similar to our three core values. These categories reflect a shift from individual delivery and component ownership to broader technical judgment, influence, and accountability for outcomes beyond one’s own work.

At this level, performance is measured not only by technical excellence, but by the Senior’s ability to guide others, make sound decisions in ambiguous situations, and positively influence team and customer outcomes. The categories are intentionally broad and are designed to support meaningful reflection and discussion during reviews rather than act as a checklist.

Consistently meeting expectations across these categories demonstrates sustained senior-level capability and readiness for increased scope, leadership, or complexity where applicable.

#### Competence in Craft

[](https://entelectza.sharepoint.com/sites/entelectintranet/SitePages/SeniorSoftwareEngineer.aspx#competence-in-craft "Permalink for Competence in Craft")

A Senior is trusted by those around them and perceived as a professional, competent member of a team. They have a reputation within the team for their technical strength and ability in craft. They know what is required of themselves and what they need to deliver. People do not question a Senior's intent or credibility. A Senior can make good decisions without the need for full context. When a Senior is given context, they can discover and prioritize new problems to solve. A Senior is not solely focused on the tasks but rather the broader context and strategy. A Senior is self-aware and humble about their strengths and where they need assistance. They are confident in their abilities and are assertive with their decision-making.

A Senior is considered the person who will contribute a lot of value with the least amount of effort. They manage and prioritize tasks in a way that effectively uses available time. They are relied upon when the team encounters problems. A Senior is pragmatic, methodical, and measured in their approach to: 

-  Their problem-solving ability which has been matured through exposure to different problems and situations. 
    
- Tackling new work as they balance innovation, quality, and the value they are bringing to their customer.
    
- Being able to advise when a solution should be pursued or avoided and considers the history that has influenced where the project is currently at.
    
- Knowledge of best practices and when they are used, at both the macro and micro level.
    
- Their delivery, due to their experience and being able to identify patterns with little detail and asking the right questions.
    

A Senior can define scope and can estimate for themselves and others. They can contribute, mentor, assist and manage their own delivery and that of others. A Senior can be tasked to drive the engagement on a small deliverable, customer, or project. They check the quality produced by other team members and display a level of responsibility for the quality of the overall project.

A Senior takes initiative and does not wait to be given tasks. They are self-managing. They stop negative sentiment in favour of finding a solution. They put ego aside when it comes to their proposed solutions. They continually improve their environment and ecosystem. 

Seniors learn and pick up new frameworks, methodologies, or tools relevant to their craft on their own with minimal assistance from others, without impacting project timelines. They look for opportunities to step out of their comfort zone and learn a new aspect of their craft, both technically and through soft skills.

#### Responsibility to Others

[](https://entelectza.sharepoint.com/sites/entelectintranet/SitePages/SeniorSoftwareEngineer.aspx#responsibility-to-others "Permalink for Responsibility to Others")

A Senior is trusted in their team and is an anchor in their craft. The team and customer rely on their experience and insight to ensure that the right things are being done. They have enough experience to share their knowledge with others and actively mentor. A Senior encourages a positive outlook, even in adversity. They approach conflict with the mindset of creating a win-win outcome for all parties.

A Senior will contribute to their broader team by: 

- Helping to motivate the other members of the team.
    
- Contributing to the overall team identity and culture.
    
- Being aware of what teammates are doing, struggling with, and how to help them.
    
- Temporarily taking on additional responsibility to assist their Lead, Line Manager or fellow team members when required.
    
- Proactively finding ways to get involved and bring additional value.
    

Occasionally, a Senior is expected to take ownership of the delivery on a small stream or project, including:

- Managing the expectations of fellow team members and the customer. 
    
- Negotiating and influencing the decisions made around the requirements and scope.
    
- Taking all administrative responsibilities from the Lead regarding that deliverable.
    
- Lead meetings and project delivery ceremonies.
    
- Establishing a well-defined and well-understood delivery approach.
    

A Senior maintains a calm demeanour and does not let personal circumstance impact their decision-making ability, delivery or allow these to impact the team. They can communicate their thought process and decisions to varying audiences clearly. They will use the appropriate language for the audience, i.e. not be overly technical with business stakeholders. A Senior will understand when it is appropriate to concede for the better of the project, team or customer. They can negotiate and explain a way forward (priorities, decisions, definition of scope) when there are differences of opinion.

#### Impact on Customer Success

[](https://entelectza.sharepoint.com/sites/entelectintranet/SitePages/SeniorSoftwareEngineer.aspx#impact-on-customer-success "Permalink for Impact on Customer Success")

A Senior derives value from their work being used in production and benefiting their customer. 

They understand that their job is more than just their craft contribution. They are aware of the how their deliverables serve the customer's needs. When there is a lack of clarity in tasks, a Senior does not blame others but pursues all collaborative avenues to find it.

A Senior makes a customer feel comfortable because their intent is to achieve the same goals. A Senior balances deadlines, requirements, quality, best practice and deliverables in order to achieve customer success, and make the customer look good. They remove the customer anxiety and fear by explaining and managing expectations. Wherever they can add value to a customer, they look to get involved.

## How to use these categories in reviews

[](https://entelectza.sharepoint.com/sites/entelectintranet/SitePages/SeniorSoftwareEngineer.aspx#how-to-use-these-categories-in-reviews "Permalink for How to use these categories in reviews")

When responding to review questions under each category, the focus should be on **observable behaviours, decision-making, impact, and outcomes over time**.

“Meeting expectations” means the individual **consistently demonstrates the behaviours, judgment, and ownership expected of a Senior Software Engineer**. “Not meeting expectations” indicates **persistent gaps in credibility, influence, delivery reliability, or impact**, despite feedback and opportunity. “Over and above expectations” is when an individual is demonstrating the behaviours and outcomes appropriate for this level, **and taking on additional tasks / behaviours once their required tasks are addressed**. 

The expectations below are provided to help conceptualise what these categories look like in practice and to create a shared understanding of what “meeting expectations” means at this level.

## What “Meeting Expectations” looks like in practice

[](https://entelectza.sharepoint.com/sites/entelectintranet/SitePages/SeniorSoftwareEngineer.aspx#what-%E2%80%9Cmeeting-expectations%E2%80%9D-looks-like-in-practice "Permalink for What “Meeting Expectations” looks like in practice")

The detailed expectations below expand on the three review categories by describing how they typically show up day-to-day through technical delivery, communication, reliability, professionalism, learning, and ownership.

They are not intended to introduce new criteria, but rather to:

- Clarify what good performance looks like at this level
    
- Make it easier to answer the longer review questions with concrete examples
    
- Provide a consistent reference point for both self-reviews and team reviews
    

Reviewers and Senior Software Engineers should use these expectations as supporting context when assessing performance within each of the three main categories.

#### Technical Judgment, Craft, and Solution Ownership

[](https://entelectza.sharepoint.com/sites/entelectintranet/SitePages/SeniorSoftwareEngineer.aspx#technical-judgment%2C-craft%2C-and-solution-ownership "Permalink for Technical Judgment, Craft, and Solution Ownership")

A Senior Software Engineer is trusted for their technical judgment and decision-making. They consistently deliver high-quality, well-tested solutions that balance technical excellence with business needs. They are able to research, evaluate, and implement technologies and approaches pragmatically, considering long-term maintainability and context.

They identify patterns quickly, ask the right questions with limited information, and are relied upon during complex troubleshooting, deployments, and incidents. Seniors can define scope, estimate work for themselves and others, and improve the accuracy of estimates over time. They may run or contribute significantly to small projects or delivery streams with minimal oversight.

They take responsibility for the quality of the broader solution, including reviewing and guiding the work of others.

- **Not meeting expectations looks like:** weak technical credibility, poor use of source control, repeated ignorance of best practices, releasing poorly tested code, inability to troubleshoot at a senior level, inability to self-manage, or being unable to clearly articulate technical decisions to peers or customers.
    

#### Ownership, Initiative, and Professional Maturity

[](https://entelectza.sharepoint.com/sites/entelectintranet/SitePages/SeniorSoftwareEngineer.aspx#ownership%2C-initiative%2C-and-professional-maturity "Permalink for Ownership, Initiative, and Professional Maturity")

Senior Software Engineers take ownership beyond their assigned tasks. They are self-managing, proactive, and continuously seek ways to improve their environment, systems, and teams. They step in where needed, particularly during high-pressure situations, and model calm, solution-oriented behaviour.

They demonstrate accountability for outcomes, put ego aside when evaluating solutions, and address problems constructively. When raising concerns, they propose realistic options and trade-offs rather than complaints.

- **Not meeting expectations looks like:** avoiding ownership, spreading negativity, failing to act when they are the most senior person present, or raising problems without contributing to solutions.
    

#### Deadlines, Delivery, and Accountability

[](https://entelectza.sharepoint.com/sites/entelectintranet/SitePages/SeniorSoftwareEngineer.aspx#deadlines%2C-delivery%2C-and-accountability "Permalink for Deadlines, Delivery, and Accountability")

At this level, Seniors are expected to be highly reliable. They consistently meet their own deadlines and, where applicable, take responsibility for delivery timelines of others they are guiding or supporting. They proactively manage risks, negotiate scope or timelines when necessary, and maintain a reputation for getting important work done.

- **Not meeting expectations looks like:** repeated failure to meet agreed timelines, poor expectation management, or lack of ownership over delivery outcomes.
    

#### Responsibility to Others, Communication, and Leadership

[](https://entelectza.sharepoint.com/sites/entelectintranet/SitePages/SeniorSoftwareEngineer.aspx#responsibility-to-others%2C-communication%2C-and-leadership "Permalink for Responsibility to Others, Communication, and Leadership")

A Senior is an anchor within their team. They are trusted advisors who mentor and guide others, contribute to a positive team culture, and support Leads or Managers when needed.

They communicate clearly and confidently with a wide range of audiences, including customers, senior stakeholders, and engineers. Seniors can resolve conflict, negotiate scope and priorities, lead meetings or ceremonies, and explain complex decisions in accessible language. They understand when to concede for the good of the project, team, or customer.

- **Not meeting expectations looks like:** poor communication leading to misunderstandings, avoidance of difficult conversations, lack of leadership presence, or failure to support and develop others.
    

#### Customer Focus and Business Impact

[](https://entelectza.sharepoint.com/sites/entelectintranet/SitePages/SeniorSoftwareEngineer.aspx#customer-focus-and-business-impact "Permalink for Customer Focus and Business Impact")

Senior Software Engineers deeply understand how their work contributes to customer success. They balance deadlines, requirements, quality, and best practices to deliver outcomes that make the customer successful and confident.

They actively manage customer expectations, remove uncertainty, and engage constructively to resolve issues around scope, priorities, and timelines. Wherever possible, they seek opportunities to add value beyond their immediate responsibilities.

- **Not meeting expectations looks like:** prioritising personal technical interests over business outcomes, poor professionalism, being unreachable when needed, or receiving repeated negative customer feedback.
    

#### Learning, Mentorship, and Growth

[](https://entelectza.sharepoint.com/sites/entelectintranet/SitePages/SeniorSoftwareEngineer.aspx#learning%2C-mentorship%2C-and-growth "Permalink for Learning, Mentorship, and Growth")

Seniors take ownership of their own growth and that of others. They independently learn new tools, frameworks, and methodologies without impacting delivery and actively contribute to training, mentorship, or knowledge-sharing initiatives.

They demonstrate continuous growth in areas identified during previous reviews and help raise the overall capability of the organisation.

- **Not meeting expectations looks like:** stagnation in skills, avoidance of learning or mentoring responsibilities, or lack of contribution to growing others.
    

#### Timekeeping and Administration

[](https://entelectza.sharepoint.com/sites/entelectintranet/SitePages/SeniorSoftwareEngineer.aspx#timekeeping-and-administration "Permalink for Timekeeping and Administration")

Senior Software Engineers model professional discipline. They manage time responsibly, meet administrative requirements consistently, and support delivery by being dependable and organised.

- **Not meeting expectations looks like:** repeated administrative lapses, unreliable time capturing, missed meetings without notice, or behaviours that undermine trust.

### Growth beyond Senior Software Engineer

[](https://entelectza.sharepoint.com/sites/entelectintranet/SitePages/SeniorSoftwareEngineer.aspx#growth-beyond-senior-software-engineer "Permalink for Growth beyond Senior Software Engineer")

Growth at a Senior Software Engineer level does not require a move into formal leadership. For many, continued growth is expressed through deepening technical expertise, strengthening judgment, expanding influence through example, and consistently delivering high-impact outcomes - all while remaining in a Senior role.

Titles follow demonstrated scope and responsibility, not tenure or expectation. As scope and influence increase, some Seniors may choose to specialise further through one of two leadership pathways, based on their strengths, interests, and the needs of the organisation.

Both leadership pathways build on strong Senior-level capability and are entered through demonstrated readiness and sustained impact. There is no expectation to pursue either path - growth is supported whether it is expressed through mastery within the Senior role or through expanded leadership scope. These two paths are also not mutually exclusive with many performing the responsibilities of both paths, while holding the title of one or the other.
