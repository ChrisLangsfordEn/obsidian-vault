​​Intermediate Software Engineers can work on their own, but still haven't built up enough experience to lead a large project or manage an account or customer independent of a senior person. They are proficient in their respective languages and should be capable of mentoring and coaching others. They may be tasked with the design of a piece of the application. They are not usually expected to consider the application as a whole unless working with senior developers unless the application is relatively small. 

They can usually do an acceptable job of troubleshooting and debugging, but they do not have enough experience to see the patterns in the problems that point them to the probable place they are occurring -they are gaining those skills and rarely need to ask for debugging help. They have probably been through the whole development cycle at least once and have seen the results of design problems and are learning how to avoid them in the future. They typically have over two years of experience. 

The Intermediate Software Engineer must strongly display all aspects required of the Software Engineer role and in addition he / she: 

- must prove that he / she is able to complete micro-tasks in the given amount of time at an acceptable level of quality.
    
- must prove that he / she understands the technologies that he / she is working on to an acceptable level, such that he / she is able to teach other engineers the high-level concepts, including but not limited to:
    
    - The fundamentals of Software Engineering (object-oriented paradigms, coding standards and practices, clean code, correct use of source control etc.)
        
    - The actual technologies that he / she is working with
        
- should be solution-focused I.e. he / she must show an active interest and gain an understanding of the business domain, business objectives and the goals of the project in the context of the customer's business.
    
- must show the ability to troubleshoot issues independently with little or no assistance from other Engineers. This is not to say they should not be asking for help but rather that they must show some problem-solving skills using their past experiences to solve minor issues.
    
- must display a positive attitude towards:
    
    - Actively learning and maximising the opportunities given to them to learn as much as possible
        
    - Work ethic and going the extra mile when necessary to pull themselves up to the level of his / her more senior peers
        
    - Personal growth across different areas of projects (coding, but also willingness to understand specifications, showing the ability to take bigger tasks and break them down themselves, demonstrating the correct level of doing things themselves and asking questions, showing signs of being self-managing)
        
    - Demonstrating a willingness to be involved in various initiatives that take him / her outside his / her comfort zone (training presentations, asking for extra work, seeing where else he / she can be useful).

## So what should an Intermediate Software Engineer focus on?

[](https://entelectza.sharepoint.com/sites/entelectintranet/SitePages/IntermediateSoftwareEngineer.aspx#so-what-should-an-intermediate-software-engineer-focus-on "Permalink for So what should an Intermediate Software Engineer focus on?")

When it comes to growth and assessing performance for Intermediate Software Engineers at this level, we focus on three core categories which you may see are similar to our three core values. These categories reflect a shift from entry-level task execution to increased independence, solution thinking, and contribution beyond one’s own delivery.

At this level, performance is not only about producing quality work, but about how effectively that work is delivered, how others are supported, and how customer outcomes are influenced. The three categories are designed to guide meaningful reflection and discussion during reviews, rather than act as a checklist.

Consistently meeting expectations across these categories indicates readiness for increased scope, complexity, and influence.

#### Competence in Craft

[](https://entelectza.sharepoint.com/sites/entelectintranet/SitePages/IntermediateSoftwareEngineer.aspx#competence-in-craft "Permalink for Competence in Craft")

An Intermediate is focused on solutioning, breaking down task problems into smaller components, solving them and then implementing them. 

At a task level, they have enough experience to be self-managed. They can do an acceptable job of troubleshooting and debugging. When troubleshooting, they do not have enough experience to see the patterns in the problems that point them to the probable place they are occurring. They are however, gaining those skills and rarely need to ask for menial craft help. 

They are starting to understand how to manage and prioritize tasks in a way that effectively uses available time. Intermediates have knowledge of best practices at the micro level and when and why they are used. They take ownership of the quality of their own work. They can define scope and can consistently estimate for themselves, not yet for others. An Intermediate has the ability to perform, be involved or own deployments. An Intermediate is able to apply past learnings to new problems and avoid repeating similar mistakes.

At a project level, they still haven't built up enough experience to lead a large project independent of a Senior or Lead. They are not usually expected to consider the solution, unless working with Seniors or Leads. They have probably been through the whole solution lifecycle at least once and seen the results of design problems, thus are learning how to avoid them in the future. 

Intermediates can learn and pick up new frameworks, methodologies, or tools relevant to their craft with the assistance of others, without impacting project timelines. Most of their learnings occur on the job. Other Seniors or Leads will direct and coach an Intermediate in finding project level opportunities to step out of their comfort zone and learn a new aspect of their craft, both technically and through soft skills.

#### Responsibility to Others

[](https://entelectza.sharepoint.com/sites/entelectintranet/SitePages/IntermediateSoftwareEngineer.aspx#responsibility-to-others "Permalink for Responsibility to Others")

An Intermediate’s responsibility is to be an active team player. Since they are proficient in their respective craft, there is an expectation of being capable of mentoring and coaching others. They are starting to hold their team members accountable and can effectively manage their expectations. 

During meetings, they will contribute when they have something valuable to say. An Intermediate maintains a calm demeanour and does not let personal circumstance impact their decision-making ability and delivery. An Intermediate does not pigeon-hole their value contribution based on personal preferences.

An Intermediate will use the appropriate language for the audience, i.e. not be overly technical with business stakeholders. They will show a sense of urgency to all team communications and will respect other people’s points of view. An Intermediate will understand when it is appropriate to concede for the better of the project, team or customer.

An Intermediate takes initiative and does not wait to be given tasks. They are known to have a positive outlook and do not perpetuate negative sentiment in the team. They are able to put their ego aside when it comes to their proposed solutions. They willingly contribute to their team (regardless of responsibility) during a time of crisis or when they can add value. An Intermediate contributes to the overall team identity and culture. 

#### Impact on Customer Success

[](https://entelectza.sharepoint.com/sites/entelectintranet/SitePages/IntermediateSoftwareEngineer.aspx#impact-on-customer-success "Permalink for Impact on Customer Success")

An Intermediate derives value from their work being used in production and benefiting the customer. They understand that their job is about their craft contribution. They are aware of how their deliverables serve the customer's needs. When there is a lack of clarity in tasks, an Intermediate doesn’t blame others but pursues all collaborative avenues to find it.

An Intermediate makes a customer feel comfortable because their intent is to achieve the same goals. An Intermediate balances deadlines, requirements, quality, best practice and deliverables in order to achieve customer success, and make the customer look good. Wherever they can add value to a customer, they look to get involved.

## How to use these categories in reviews

[](https://entelectza.sharepoint.com/sites/entelectintranet/SitePages/IntermediateSoftwareEngineer.aspx#how-to-use-these-categories-in-reviews "Permalink for How to use these categories in reviews")

When responding to the review questions under each category, the focus should be on **observable behaviours, deliverables, and growth over time**, not intent alone.

“Meeting expectations” means the individual is **reliably demonstrating the behaviours and outcomes appropriate for an intermediate level**, with limited guidance. “Not meeting expectations” indicates **persistent gaps in independence, ownership, or impact**, despite feedback and support. “Over and above expectations” is when an individual is demonstrating the behaviours and outcomes appropriate for an intermediate level **without guidance, and taking on additional tasks / behaviours** once their required tasks are addressed. 

The sections that follow provide more detailed expectations and examples. These are included to help conceptualise what each category looks like in practice and to create a shared understanding of what “meeting expectations” means for this role.

## What “Meeting Expectations” looks like in practice

[](https://entelectza.sharepoint.com/sites/entelectintranet/SitePages/IntermediateSoftwareEngineer.aspx#what-%E2%80%9Cmeeting-expectations%E2%80%9D-looks-like-in-practice "Permalink for What “Meeting Expectations” looks like in practice")

The detailed expectations below expand on the three review categories by describing how they typically show up day-to-day through technical delivery, communication, reliability, professionalism, learning, and ownership.

They are not intended to introduce new criteria, but rather to:

- Clarify what good performance looks like at this level
    
- Make it easier to answer the longer review questions with concrete examples
    
- Provide a consistent reference point for both self-reviews and team reviews
    

Reviewers and Intermediate Software Engineers should use these expectations as supporting context when assessing performance within each of the three main categories.

#### Technical Ability, Solutioning, and Craft

[](https://entelectza.sharepoint.com/sites/entelectintranet/SitePages/IntermediateSoftwareEngineer.aspx#technical-ability%2C-solutioning%2C-and-craft "Permalink for Technical Ability, Solutioning, and Craft")

An Intermediate Software Engineer is expected to work independently on assigned tasks and components, taking ownership of quality, correctness, and delivery. They can design and implement components within a system to an acceptable level, apply best practices appropriately, and balance technical considerations with business requirements.

They are able to self-manage their work, estimate their own tasks, and apply past experience to new technical challenges. They can perform, participate in, or own deployments and contribute meaningfully to issue resolution. Code releases are expected to be reliable, robust, and reasonably well tested.

They should rarely need assistance with basic troubleshooting and should demonstrate continuous improvement in how they approach recurring or menial tasks.

- **Not meeting expectations looks like:** inability to work independently, poor use of source control, repeated ignorance of best practices, releasing poorly tested code, difficulty troubleshooting issues expected at this level, or inability to self-manage assigned work.
    

#### Ownership, Initiative, and Professional Attitude

[](https://entelectza.sharepoint.com/sites/entelectintranet/SitePages/IntermediateSoftwareEngineer.aspx#ownership%2C-initiative%2C-and-professional-attitude "Permalink for Ownership, Initiative, and Professional Attitude")

An Intermediate Software Engineer is expected to take initiative, demonstrate accountability, and contribute positively to team momentum. They do not wait to be told what to do next and actively look for opportunities to add value within their scope.

They are reliable, dependable, and solution-oriented, particularly when challenges arise. They contribute constructively during high-pressure periods such as deployments or incidents and maintain a positive, professional attitude. When raising concerns, they are expected to propose realistic solutions.

- **Not meeting expectations looks like:** doing only what is explicitly asked, avoiding accountability, spreading negativity, making complaints without offering solutions, or being unreliable when the team needs support.
    

#### Deadlines and Delivery Reliability

[](https://entelectza.sharepoint.com/sites/entelectintranet/SitePages/IntermediateSoftwareEngineer.aspx#deadlines-and-delivery-reliability "Permalink for Deadlines and Delivery Reliability")

Meeting commitments is a core expectation at this level. An Intermediate Software Engineer consistently delivers work within agreed timelines and manages expectations proactively when risks emerge. They balance quality, scope, and deadlines responsibly and are known for getting work done.

- **Not meeting expectations looks like:** repeatedly missing deadlines without valid justification or failing to communicate delivery risks early.
    

#### Responsibility to Others, Communication, and Leadership

[](https://entelectza.sharepoint.com/sites/entelectintranet/SitePages/IntermediateSoftwareEngineer.aspx#responsibility-to-others%2C-communication%2C-and-leadership "Permalink for Responsibility to Others, Communication, and Leadership")

Intermediates are expected to contribute beyond their own delivery by supporting others. This includes mentoring and coaching junior engineers, sharing knowledge, and being a trusted source of guidance.

They communicate clearly and appropriately for their audience, keep relevant stakeholders informed, and can engage confidently with customer's (for example, running demos or discussing progress). They remain calm under pressure, respect differing viewpoints, and put the success of the team and project ahead of personal preferences or ego.

- **Not meeting expectations looks like:** poor communication leading to misunderstandings, only communicating when prompted, avoiding difficult conversations, or not being trusted to support or guide junior team members.
    

#### Customer Focus and Business Impact

[](https://entelectza.sharepoint.com/sites/entelectintranet/SitePages/IntermediateSoftwareEngineer.aspx#customer-focus-and-business-impact "Permalink for Customer Focus and Business Impact")

An Intermediate Software Engineer understands how their work contributes to customer success. They take business objectives into account when designing and implementing solutions, balancing technical decisions with deadlines, requirements, and outcomes.

They behave professionally in all customer interactions and seek opportunities to add value where appropriate.

- **Not meeting expectations looks like:** prioritising personal technical interests over business outcomes, being unreachable when needed, showing poor professionalism, or receiving repeated negative customer feedback.
    

#### Learning, Mentorship, and Growth

[](https://entelectza.sharepoint.com/sites/entelectintranet/SitePages/IntermediateSoftwareEngineer.aspx#learning%2C-mentorship%2C-and-growth "Permalink for Learning, Mentorship, and Growth")

Growth at this level includes deepening technical skills while increasingly supporting the growth of others. Intermediates are expected to actively upskill through available learning channels and to contribute to training or knowledge-sharing initiatives where possible.

They should show clear progress in areas highlighted in previous reviews.

- **Not meeting expectations looks like:** limited evidence of skill growth, avoidance of learning opportunities, or failure to mentor or support others.
    

#### Timekeeping and Administration

[](https://entelectza.sharepoint.com/sites/entelectintranet/SitePages/IntermediateSoftwareEngineer.aspx#timekeeping-and-administration "Permalink for Timekeeping and Administration")

Professional administration remains an expectation. This includes accurate and on-time timesheet capturing, attending scheduled meetings, and managing working hours responsibly.

- **Not meeting expectations looks like:** frequent missed timesheet capturing deadlines, unclear or questionable time entries, repeated missed meetings without notice, or a reputation for clock-watching.
