The Solutions Architect is expected to come up with new ideas around the improvement of their personal brand, the company’s brand and the technical level of Entelectuals. The Solutions Architect will also be required to plan, budget, rollout, execute and run these ideas rather than delegate everything out to the Technical Leads.

## Competence in Craft

[](https://entelectza.sharepoint.com/sites/entelectintranet/SitePages/SolutionsArchitect.aspx#competence-in-craft "Permalink for Competence in Craft")

A Solution Architect has a strong technical foundation and broad knowledge across core coding disciplines. While they may not contribute code daily, their credibility is built on understanding how code quality, patterns, and decisions influence long-term sustainability. Their experience allows them to evaluate multiple technical options, assess trade-offs, and recommend the technical direction that best supports the customer's long-term goals.

A Solution Architect has been exposed to different system types, domains, and technical environments. Their value is rooted in maturity, holistic thinking, and clarity of reasoning. They ensure systems are built with durability and quality in mind, and can guide teams to follow sound technical principles.

They frequently meet with customers and senior stakeholders to clarify direction, align expectations, and provide a clear architectural vision. A Solution Architect “paints the picture” of how the system should work at a high level, and communicates this vision in a structured, simple, and executable manner. People trust their intent because they think objectively, make informed decisions, and articulate their reasoning with confidence and composure.

## Responsibility to Others

[](https://entelectza.sharepoint.com/sites/entelectintranet/SitePages/SolutionsArchitect.aspx#responsibility-to-others "Permalink for Responsibility to Others")

A Solution Architect supports developers, leads, customers, and other stakeholders by elevating their perspective beyond day-to-day delivery. They bring alignment to the technical approach, quality standards, and the long-term shape of the system. They challenge assumptions responsibly, ask the right questions, and ensure that the reasoning behind decisions is understood by both internal teams and customer representatives.

They guide teams and customers by identifying technical gaps that could introduce long-term risk. A Solution Architect does not simply fix issues; they help others understand why the gap matters, how it affects delivery and customer outcomes, and how to approach similar challenges differently in the future.

Their focus is on technical mentorship. They share insight, explain technical reasoning, and enable better practices and patterns. They help people think beyond the immediate task and consider long-term sustainability for the customer’s system. Their influence comes from expertise rather than authority, and they encourage questions, exploration, and responsible growth rather than directing through instruction.

A Solution Architect maintains composure and clear thinking, even under delivery pressure or when navigating differing views from customers. They communicate with intention, tailor their language to suit either technical or customer needs, and break down complex challenges into simple, actionable guidance. In doing so, they make technical problems feel approachable and enable both teams and customers to deepen their understanding of sustainable long-term technical decision-making.

## Impact on Customer Success 

[](https://entelectza.sharepoint.com/sites/entelectintranet/SitePages/SolutionsArchitect.aspx#impact-on-customer-success "Permalink for Impact on Customer Success")

A Solution Architect derives value not only from features being delivered, but from the long-term quality, sustainability, and evolution of the customer’s system. They bring sound judgment into discussions and act as a steady voice that gives customers confidence.

They ensure customers understand the technical implications of decisions, guide them toward architectural choices that align to strategic objectives, and protect them from short-term decisions that may cause long-term harm. A Solution Architect balances requirements, deadlines, quality and best practice in order to achieve customer success.

They remove customer anxiety by explaining trade-offs, managing perceptions, and providing clear feedback loops. Customers trust their guidance because they are consistent, transparent, and invested in long-term outcomes rather than short-term shortcuts.

By maintaining architectural integrity, preserving technical vision, and raising system quality, the Solution Architect strengthens customer trust and contributes directly to sustained customer success.
