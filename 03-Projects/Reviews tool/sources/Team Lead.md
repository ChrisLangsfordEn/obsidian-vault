
A Team Lead is responsible for the health of delivery, the growth of people, and the strength of the client relationship.

They operate at the intersection of three responsibilities:

- Client outcomes – ensuring work delivered meets business objectives, quality expectations and timelines.
    
    - Meeting delivery expectations
        
    - Building trust and credibility
        
    - Acting as a professional representative of Entelect
        
- Team effectiveness – creating an environment where individuals feel supported, clear on expectations, and able to grow.
    
    - Supporting growth, morale and wellbeing
        
    - Creating clarity and direction
        
    - Enabling individuals to perform at their best
        
- Entelect contribution – representing the business professionally, upholding values, and contributing beyond the immediate project.
    
    - Upholding company values
        
    - Contributing beyond the immediate project
        
    - Demonstrating ownership and professionalism
        

Performance is assessed across both results (what gets delivered) and behaviours (how leadership is demonstrated). A strong Team Lead balances these areas daily. This often requires putting the needs of the client, team, or business above personal convenience, particularly in moments of pressure, ambiguity or complexity.

## So what should a Team Lead focus on?

[](https://entelectza.sharepoint.com/sites/entelectintranet/SitePages/TeamLead.aspx#so-what-should-a-team-lead-focus-on "Permalink for So what should a Team Lead focus on?")

When it comes to growth and assessing performance for Team Leads, we focus on three core categories which you may see are similar to our three core values. These categories reflect the shift from individual contribution to leadership impact - including creating clarity and direction for others, strengthening delivery across teams, influencing stakeholders, and taking accountability for outcomes that extend beyond one’s own work.

At this level, performance is assessed across both results (what gets delivered) and behaviours (how leadership is demonstrated). The categories are intentionally broad and are designed to support meaningful reflection and discussion during reviews rather than act as a checklist.

Consistently meeting expectations across these categories demonstrates sustained senior-level capability and readiness for increased scope, leadership, or complexity where applicable.

#### Competence in Craft 

[](https://entelectza.sharepoint.com/sites/entelectintranet/SitePages/TeamLead.aspx#competence-in-craft "Permalink for Competence in Craft")

A Lead is trusted by those around them and perceived as a professional, competent member of a team. They are hands-on craft people with strong technical knowledge that have the added responsibility of balancing team needs, customer needs and delivery. The value that a Lead will bring is no longer heavily weighted to their technical craft but includes their people leadership and management.

They know what is required of the team and what needs to be delivered. People do not question a Lead's intent or credibility. A Lead is able to make good decisions without the need for full context. A Lead is able to seek the required context and impart this onto the team. They are focused on high level objectives/broader delivery goals and consistently guide the team. A Lead is self-aware and humble about their strengths and where they need assistance. They are confident in their abilities and are assertive with their decision-making.

A Lead is required to guide the team through problems and times of crisis. A Lead is able to identify project and team risks and work to resolve or mitigate these risks before they impact delivery.

A Lead ensures that the team follows a well-defined delivery approach to requirements, estimations, scope, task management, change management, risk management, release management, design and architecture, quality assurance, project planning and status reporting.

A Lead will be supportive and will be there for the team. They are able to put ego aside when it comes to their decisions and will understand that their point of view will not always be correct. A Lead is able influence change and lead even if they do not have explicit authority. They own their decision-making process and do not delegate decision or the ownership of their team’s affairs

A Lead can learn and pick up new technical and soft skills on their own without assistance from others. Learning these skills comes from investing time into reading up about their new craft, learning on the job and networking. They look for opportunities to step out of their comfort zone and learn new aspects of their traditional craft, management and leadership.

#### Responsibility to others

[](https://entelectza.sharepoint.com/sites/entelectintranet/SitePages/TeamLead.aspx#responsibility-to-others "Permalink for Responsibility to others")

The team and customer rely on a Lead’s experience and insight to ensure that the right things are being done. They have enough experience to share their knowledge with others. A Lead encourages a positive outlook, even in adversity. They approach conflict with the mindset of creating a win-win outcome for all parties. A Lead assumes full responsibility for the team’s deliverables and projects.

A Lead’s benevolent intent towards the team is to help their team members grow as professionals. They understand that by making their team members word-class, delivery results will be world-class. A Lead mentors and coaches directly or by delegating the responsibility when appropriate. They do this through mechanisms such as:

- Having awareness of the team’s affairs (strengths, weaknesses, goals, concerns, tasks, workload, limits and achievements).
    
-  Setting the correct expectations and then holding their team members accountable.
    
-  Enabling team members to experiment, try or step out of their comfort zone.
    
- Responsibly pushing their team members into new growth areas and knowing when this is appropriate.
    
- Conducting regular one-on-ones and providing meaningful review sessions.
    
- Soliciting and providing valuable feedback for team members.
    
- Understanding what the base line expectations for all the different roles and crafts at Entelect are.
    

A Lead is required to develop their own leadership style to facilitate positive sentiment and a strong team identity. They make themselves available to have ad hoc face-to-face sessions when team members need it. They support the team when required. They are able motivate and energise the team. When a Lead is required to take action, but is unsure or lacks the authority, they do not defer or delegate the responsibility, they take ownership, and work with the right people to solve the problem. By ensuring a positive and safe team environment, the team members are able to focus on maintaining a strong delivery track record. A Lead is expected to onboard new team members, making them feel comfortable, excited, and able to deliver quickly. They are also expected to offboard team members, making sure the new Lead gains all relevant insight and history, ensuring consistent career growth.

A Lead maintains a calm demeanour and does not let personal circumstance impact their decision-making ability, delivery or allow these to impact the team. They can communicate their thought process and decisions to varying audiences clearly. They will use the appropriate language for the audience, i.e. not be overly technical with business stakeholders. A Lead will understand when it is appropriate to concede for the better of the project, team or customer. They can negotiate and explain a way forward (priorities, decisions, definition of scope) when there are differences of opinion.

#### Impact on Customer Success

[](https://entelectza.sharepoint.com/sites/entelectintranet/SitePages/TeamLead.aspx#impact-on-customer-success "Permalink for Impact on Customer Success")

A Lead derives value from the team’s work being used in production and benefiting the customer. They are aware of the how the team’s deliverables serve the customer’s needs. When there is a lack of clarity in tasks, a Lead doesn’t blame others but pursues all collaborative avenues to find it.

A Lead makes a customer feel comfortable because their intent is to achieve the same goals. A Lead balances deadlines, requirements, quality, best practice and deliverables in order to achieve customer success, and make the customer look good. They remove the customer anxiety and fear by explaining and managing expectations and perceptions. They provide consistent feedback loops to the customer. Wherever they can add value to a customer, they look to get involved.

A Lead is cognisant of the economics and engagement model at their respective customer, ensuring the customer is getting the value for the cost in time or effort. They strive for their team to be seen as an asset. They assume the responsibility on behalf of everyone on their team and become the point person for the customer.

Leads are trusted advisors and are respected by their customers. They are proactive in building relationships with the senior members of the customer.

## How to use these categories in reviews

[](https://entelectza.sharepoint.com/sites/entelectintranet/SitePages/TeamLead.aspx#how-to-use-these-categories-in-reviews "Permalink for How to use these categories in reviews")

When responding to review questions under each category, the focus should be on **observable behaviours, decision-making, impact, and outcomes over time**.

For the Team Lead role, “meeting expectations” means **consistently demonstrating the leadership behaviours, sound judgement, and level of ownership** expected at this level. This includes taking accountability for team outcomes, influencing effectively, and maintaining credibility with team members, clients, and stakeholders.

“Not meeting expectations” reflects **ongoing gaps in leadership impact, delivery reliability, influence, or professional credibility**, particularly where these gaps persist despite feedback, support, and opportunity to improve.

“Over and above expectations” describes **performance where the Team Lead is not only delivering strongly against the core expectations of the role, but is also extending their impact** - proactively strengthening the wider environment, supporting others beyond their immediate responsibilities, and contributing at a broader organisational level once their primary accountabilities are well managed.

The expectations outlined below are intended to help illustrate what these performance levels look like in practice and to build a shared understanding of what “meeting expectations” means for a Team Lead.

## What “Meeting Expectations” looks like in practice

[](https://entelectza.sharepoint.com/sites/entelectintranet/SitePages/TeamLead.aspx#what-%E2%80%9Cmeeting-expectations%E2%80%9D-looks-like-in-practice "Permalink for What “Meeting Expectations” looks like in practice")

The detailed expectations below expand on the three review categories by describing how they typically show up day-to-day through technical delivery, communication, reliability, professionalism, learning, and ownership.

They are not intended to introduce new criteria, but rather to:

- Clarify what good performance looks like at this level
    
- Make it easier to answer the longer review questions with concrete examples
    
- Provide a consistent reference point for both self-reviews and team reviews
    

Reviewers and Team Leads should use these expectations as supporting context when assessing performance within each of the three main categories.

#### Delivery Ownership & Execution

[](https://entelectza.sharepoint.com/sites/entelectintranet/SitePages/TeamLead.aspx#delivery-ownership-execution "Permalink for Delivery Ownership & Execution")

A Team Lead applies a structured and repeatable approach to delivery, maintains clear visibility of progress, risks and obstacles, and takes accountability for timelines, technical quality, usability and business outcomes. They manage expectations with clients and stakeholders through regular feedback loops and transparent communication.

They also build delivery approaches that scale across projects and environments, proactively identifies and drives improvements in process, quality or execution, makes sound decisions quickly under uncertainty, and is recognised as someone who strengthens delivery beyond the boundaries of their own team.

- **Not meeting expectations looks like:** frequently missed deadlines or inconsistent quality, lack visibility into progress or risks, avoid accountability when delivery falls short and delivery issues negatively affect client trust or Entelect’s reputation.
    

#### Technical & Problem-Solving Capability

[](https://entelectza.sharepoint.com/sites/entelectintranet/SitePages/TeamLead.aspx#technical-problem-solving-capability "Permalink for Technical & Problem-Solving Capability")

A Team Lead demonstrates a solid understanding of the business domain and project context, makes appropriate technical decisions and can explain them clearly, troubleshoots effectively while knowing when to seek support, and shows practical understanding of architecture, constraints and trade-offs.

A Team Lead quickly grasps complex domains and guides technical direction early, designs solutions that align business needs with technical implementation, demonstrates strong systems thinking and attention to detail, and acts as a technical sounding board for others.

- **Not meeting expectations looks like:** making poor technical decisions that leads to rework or failure, struggling to articulate work clearly to clients or stakeholders, and struggling to diagnose and resolve problems effectively.
    

#### Leadership & Team Environment

[](https://entelectza.sharepoint.com/sites/entelectintranet/SitePages/TeamLead.aspx#leadership-team-environment "Permalink for Leadership & Team Environment")

A Team Lead builds trust with team members, leads by example in behaviour and professionalism, creates clarity around expectations and direction, encourages participation in Entelect initiatives, and supports the team during high-pressure periods.

They are also respected by team, peers, managers and clients, they build strong cohesion, motivation and accountability, they shield the team from unnecessary interference, and actively develops emerging leaders within the team.

- **Not meeting expectations looks like:** a team that lacks direction or motivation, weak relationships with team members, team members who are disengaged or hesitant to raise issues.
    

#### Client Partnership & Professional Impact

[](https://entelectza.sharepoint.com/sites/entelectintranet/SitePages/TeamLead.aspx#client-partnership-professional-impact "Permalink for Client Partnership & Professional Impact")

A Team Lead understands the client’s business objectives, builds professional relationships with key stakeholders, acts with professionalism in communication, conduct and reliability, and proactively identifies opportunities to add value through process improvements, architectural insight, performance enhancements or training.

A Team Lead who is exceeding expectations is seen as a trusted advisor by senior client stakeholders, influences client decisions around technology, process or strategy, and actively grows the account by identifying opportunities for Entelect.

- **Not meeting expectations looks like:** poor client feedback, weak relationships with stakeholders, prioritising personal technical interests over business outcomes, missed opportunities to add value.
    

#### Communication & Influence

[](https://entelectza.sharepoint.com/sites/entelectintranet/SitePages/TeamLead.aspx#communication-influence "Permalink for Communication & Influence")

A Team Lead communicates clearly, honestly and constructively, proactively shares updates, risks, achievements and concerns, adapts communication style based on audience and context, and practices active listening by asking thoughtful clarifying questions.

A Team Lead who is exceeding expectations navigates difficult conversations with maturity and impact, accurately reads team and client sentiment, creates an environment of openness and psychological safety, and resolves conflict effectively.

- **Not meeting expectations looks like:** Important information reaching stakeholders via others, communication is reactive rather than proactive, poor clarity leads to misunderstandings, and difficult conversations are avoided.
    

#### Growth, Coaching & Ownership of Development

[](https://entelectza.sharepoint.com/sites/entelectintranet/SitePages/TeamLead.aspx#growth%2C-coaching-ownership-of-development "Permalink for Growth, Coaching & Ownership of Development")

A Team Lead provides constructive and honest feedback regularly, holds meaningful development conversations with team members, encourages learning and participation in training initiatives, and demonstrates growth against previous feedback.

They take a structured approach to developing people, is known as a strong coach and mentor within the organisation, actively identifies potential and grows future leaders, and shows strong personal growth across multiple capability areas.

- **Not meeting expectations looks like:** avoiding development conversations, showing little improvement on repeated feedback, and they do not meaningfully invest in team growth
    

#### Professional Discipline (Reliability, Admin & Ways of Working)

[](https://entelectza.sharepoint.com/sites/entelectintranet/SitePages/TeamLead.aspx#professional-discipline-\(reliability%2C-admin-ways-of-working\) "Permalink for Professional Discipline (Reliability, Admin & Ways of Working)")

A Team Lead consistently meets deadlines, captures time accurately and reliably, attends meetings and commitments dependably, and manages administrative responsibilities responsibly.

A Team Lead is highly dependable across all responsibilities, proactively supports others when needed, and demonstrates strong ownership of operational excellence.

- **Not meeting expectations looks like:** frequently miss deadlines or meetings, poor timesheet capturing, and being seen as inconsistent or unreliable
